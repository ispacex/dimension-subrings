# pq2048 — towards order $2^{11}$: pruning whole subtrees

Русская версия: [README.ru.md](README.ru.md).

Setup: a vertex $R$ of the 2-group generation tree may be cut only by a condition that (a) is
necessary for the parent of a minimal counterexample $Q$ by the lemma in
[`../pq1024/README.md`](../pq1024/README.md), or for any of its quotient ancestors, and (b) passes
to quotients, so that there are no candidates below a cut vertex. The condition must be proved;
one checked only on data will not do.

Here: the proved conditions (Theorems 1 and 2), their check on orders 32–512, how much they cut,
an estimate for $2^{11}$ and a conclusion about the setup. **The result in short.**

* Two conditions that pass to quotients are proved: the rank condition (Г) and the "order
  budget" (Г$_N$) for the search of candidates of order $2^N$. (The labels Г, Г$_N$ are those used
  in the code comments.) Both rest on one lemma: for a group of class 3 with cyclic centre the
  commutator gives a non-degenerate pairing
  $\gamma_2/(\gamma_2 \cap Z) \times Q/C_Q(\gamma_2) \to \gamma_3$.
* Nothing is lost: the search with pruning gives the same candidates, 3 / 11 / 64 / 308 on orders
  64–512, by rank and line by line; the theorems are checked on all ancestors of all these
  candidates.
* (Г) cuts nothing on orders up to $2^{10}$ (and this is seen from the inequalities). (Г$_N$) cuts
  a lot, but mostly the parents themselves: on order 512 about 4 % of them remain, and the parents
  of order $2^{10}$ for $2^{11}$ are, by a sample, $10^5$–$2 \cdot 10^6$ instead of
  $0.5$–$1.1 \cdot 10^9$.
* What stays expensive is generation: expanding ≈ 9 million vertices of order 512 (mostly of
  $p$-class 2, ranks 5 and 6) — ≈ 100 CPU-days by the sample (section "Estimate for $2^{11}$"), of
  which ≈ 85 % is rank 5 (rank 6 is underestimated there: without the step rules (`steps.g`) it
  would cost ≈ 240 days, with them about a day). The proposal is to close $2^{11}$ for ranks
  $\ne 5$ (≈ 2–3 CPU-days with the step rules, ranks 2–4 a day), and rank 5 after proving
  statement 1 of "Not proved" (section "Conclusion"). Ranks 2 and 3 are already done: 603
  candidates of order $2^{11}$, all with $D_4 = D_5 = 1$.

## Notation

A *candidate* is a finite 2-group $Q$ of class 3 with conditions (1)–(4) of the lemma: the centre
$Z(Q)$ is cyclic, its involution $n$ lies in $\gamma_3(Q) \cap M_5(Q)$, the $2$-class is $c \ge 4$,
$P_c(Q) = \langle n \rangle$, the parent is $R = Q/P_c(Q)$. Here $P_1 = Q$,
$P_{i+1} = [P_i, Q] P_i^2$ (`PCentralSeries(Q, 2)`), $P_c \ne 1 = P_{c+1}$. The ancestors of $R$
in the tree are $Q/P_k(Q)$, $2 \le k \le c$; all of them are quotients of $Q$ by subgroups of
$\Phi(Q) = P_2(Q)$, and all have rank $d = d(Q)$.

Below only these are used: class 3, $Z(Q)$ cyclic, $n \in \gamma_3(Q)$. The condition
$n \in M_5$ plays no part in the theorems (it does not pass to quotients: it is a condition "from
below").

$d = d(Q)$, $r_{\max}(d) = \min(d, d(d-1)/2)$, $Q_{ab} = Q/\gamma_2(Q)$. For a finite abelian
2-group $H \cong \bigoplus_j \mathbb Z/2^{\lambda_j}$, $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots$, the
*type* is $(\lambda_j)$, the rank $\mathrm{rk}\,H$ is the number of summands. The *join* of types
$H_1 \vee H_2$ is the group with $\lambda_j = \max(\lambda_j(H_1), \lambda_j(H_2))$.

## The pairing lemma

**Lemma A.** Let $Q$ be a 2-group of class 3 with cyclic centre (the proof needs only that
$\gamma_3(Q)$ is cyclic), $C = C_Q(\gamma_2(Q))$, $X = \gamma_2(Q)/(\gamma_2(Q) \cap Z(Q))$. Then
$X \cong Q/C$, $Q/C$ is a quotient of $Q_{ab}$, and $\mathrm{rk}\,X \le r_{\max}(d)$.

*Proof.* Class 3: $[\gamma_2, \gamma_2] \le \gamma_4 = 1$, so $\gamma_2$ is abelian and
$\gamma_2 \le C$; $\gamma_3 \le Z(Q)$, and $\gamma_3$ is cyclic as a subgroup of the cyclic
$Z(Q)$, $|\gamma_3| = 2^e$. For $y \in \gamma_2$, $x \in Q$ the commutator $[y, x]$ lies in the
central $\gamma_3$, hence
$[yy', x] = [y, x]^{y'}[y', x] = [y,x][y',x]$ and $[y, xx'] = [y, x'][y, x]^{x'} = [y,x][y,x']$:
the map $(y, x) \mapsto [y, x]$ is bilinear $\gamma_2 \times Q \to \gamma_3$. The left kernel is
$\gamma_2 \cap Z(Q)$, the right one is $C$, so we get a non-degenerate pairing
$X \times Q/C \to \gamma_3 \cong \mathbb Z/2^e$. Hence $X \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}(Q/C, \mathbb Z/2^e)$
and $Q/C \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}(X, \mathbb Z/2^e)$; for a finite abelian $B$,
$|\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb Z/2^e)| \le |B|$ with equality only when $\exp B \mid 2^e$, and then
$\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb Z/2^e) \cong B$. So $|X| = |Q/C|$ and $X \cong Q/C$. Since
$\gamma_2 \le C$, $Q/C$ is a quotient of $Q_{ab}$ and is generated by $d$ elements. On the other
hand, $\gamma_2 = \langle [x_i, x_j] \rangle \gamma_3$ for generators $x_1, \dots, x_d$ (the
commutator is bilinear modulo $\gamma_3$), and $\gamma_3 \le \gamma_2 \cap Z$; hence $X$ is
generated by $d(d-1)/2$ elements. $\square$

**Lemma B (types).** If a finite abelian 2-group $H$ maps onto $H_1$ and onto $H_2$, then
$\lambda_j(H) \ge \max(\lambda_j(H_1), \lambda_j(H_2))$ for all $j$, and
$|H| \ge |H_1 \vee H_2|$.

*Proof.* $H \twoheadrightarrow H_i$ implies $2^t H \twoheadrightarrow 2^t H_i$, and the rank does
not grow under passing to a quotient: $\#\{j : \lambda_j(H) > t\} = \mathrm{rk}\,2^tH \ge
\mathrm{rk}\,2^tH_i = \#\{j : \lambda_j(H_i) > t\}$ for all $t \ge 0$. This is exactly the
componentwise inequality of the ordered $\lambda$. $\square$

## Theorem 1: condition (Г)

**Condition (Г)** for a 2-group $V$ of class $\le 3$ and rank $d$: there is a cyclic subgroup
$K$ with $\gamma_3(V) \le K \le \gamma_2(V) \cap Z(V)$ and $\mathrm{rk}(\gamma_2(V)/K) \le r_{\max}(d)$.

**Theorem 1.** If $Q$ is a candidate and $N \trianglelefteq Q$, $N \le \Phi(Q)$, then $V = Q/N$
satisfies (Г). In particular, (Г) holds for $R$, for all its ancestors in the tree and for $Q$
itself.

*Proof.* $d(V) = d(Q)$ since $N \le \Phi(Q)$. Take $K = (\gamma_2(Q) \cap Z(Q))N/N$. It is cyclic
(the image of a subgroup of the cyclic $Z(Q)$), central, lies in $\gamma_2(V) = \gamma_2(Q)N/N$
and contains $\gamma_3(V) = \gamma_3(Q)N/N$, since $\gamma_3(Q) \le \gamma_2(Q) \cap Z(Q)$.
Finally, $\gamma_2(V)/K$ is a quotient of $X$, and
$\mathrm{rk}(\gamma_2(V)/K) \le \mathrm{rk}\,X \le r_{\max}(d)$ by Lemma A. $\square$

*Corollaries* (checked without enumerating $K$): (Г3) $\mathrm{rk}\,\gamma_2(V) \le r_{\max} + 1$;
(Г4) $\mathrm{rk}(\gamma_2(V)/(\gamma_2(V) \cap Z(V))) \le r_{\max}$.

*Passing to quotients* (property (b)): if $V$ satisfies (Г) with the subgroup $K$ and
$M \le \Phi(V)$, then $V/M$ satisfies (Г) with the subgroup $KM/M$ (the same argument: images of
cyclic and central subgroups, the rank of a quotient is not larger). So the set of vertices with
(Г) is closed upwards in the tree, and cutting by (Г) loses nothing.

## Theorem 2: condition (Г$_N$), the order budget

**Lemma C.** Let $Q$ be a 2-group of class 3 with cyclic $\gamma_3(Q)$ of order $2^e \ge 2$. Then
$Q_{ab}$ has at least two invariants $\ge e$ ($\lambda_2(Q_{ab}) \ge e$), and $X$ (Lemma A, if the
centre is cyclic) contains an element of order $\ge 2^e$.

*Proof.* $\gamma_3 = [\gamma_2, Q]$ is generated by the commutators $[[u,v],w]$ (the map
$g \mapsto [g, w]$ is a homomorphism on $\gamma_2$, see Lemma A). A cyclic 2-group generated by a
set is generated by one of its elements (the subgroups form a chain), so
$\gamma_3 = \langle [x,y,z] \rangle$. For fixed $z$ the map $(u, v) \mapsto [u, v, z]$ is well
defined on $Q_{ab} \times Q_{ab}$, bilinear and alternating (class 3:
$[ug, v] = [u,v][u,v,g][g,v]$, $[u,v,g] \in \gamma_4 = 1$, $[g,v] \in \gamma_3$ is central).
Hence it factors through $\Lambda^2 Q_{ab} \cong \bigoplus_{i<j} \mathbb Z/2^{\min(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)}$,
whose exponent is $2^{\lambda_2}$; and the image contains an element of order $2^e$. The element
$[x,y](\gamma_2 \cap Z) \in X$ pairs with $zC$ to a generator of $\gamma_3$, so its order is at
least $2^e$. $\square$

**Condition (Г$_N$)** for $V$ of rank $d$, $2^{e_V} = |\gamma_3(V)|$: there is $K$ as in
condition (Г) for which
$$2\,|K|\,\bigl|A \vee B \vee E_1\bigr|\,\bigl|A \vee V_{ab} \vee E_2\bigr| \le 2^N,$$
where $A = \gamma_2(V)/K$, $B = V/C_V(\gamma_2(V))$, $E_1 = \mathbb Z/2^{e_V+1}$, $E_2 = E_1^2$.

**Theorem 2.** If $Q$ is a candidate of order $2^N$ and $N_0 \trianglelefteq Q$,
$n \in N_0 \le \Phi(Q)$ (for example, $N_0 = P_k(Q)$, $2 \le k \le c$: all ancestors of $R$ and
$R$ itself), then $V = Q/N_0$ satisfies (Г$_N$).

*Proof.* Let $K$ be the same as in Theorem 1, $X$ and $C$ as in Lemma A, $2^e = |\gamma_3(Q)|$.
Since $n \in \gamma_3(Q) \cap N_0$, $|\gamma_3(Q)| \ge 2|\gamma_3(V)|$, that is, $e \ge e_V + 1$.
Further, $|Q| = |Q_{ab}|\,|\gamma_2(Q)|$ and $|\gamma_2(Q)| = |X|\,|\gamma_2(Q) \cap Z(Q)|$.

* $X \twoheadrightarrow A$ (by the definition of $K$); $X \cong Q/C \twoheadrightarrow V/(CN_0/N_0)
  \twoheadrightarrow B$, since the image of $C$ centralizes $\gamma_2(V)$; $X \twoheadrightarrow E_1$
  by Lemma C. By Lemma B, $|X| \ge |A \vee B \vee E_1|$.
* $|\gamma_2(Q) \cap Z(Q)| = |K| \cdot |\gamma_2(Q) \cap Z(Q) \cap N_0| \ge 2|K|$, since
  $n \in \gamma_3(Q) \le \gamma_2(Q) \cap Z(Q)$ and $n \in N_0$.
* $Q_{ab} \twoheadrightarrow Q/C \cong X \twoheadrightarrow A$, $Q_{ab} \twoheadrightarrow V_{ab}$ and
  $Q_{ab} \twoheadrightarrow E_2$ (Lemma C, $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge e \ge e_V + 1$). By
  Lemma B, $|Q_{ab}| \ge |A \vee V_{ab} \vee E_2|$.

Multiplying, $2^N = |Q| \ge 2|K|\,|A \vee B \vee E_1|\,|A \vee V_{ab} \vee E_2|$. $\square$

*Passing to quotients:* under $V \to V/M$, $M \le \Phi(V)$, the subgroup $K$ goes to $KM/M$ (not
larger), $A$, $B$ and $V_{ab}$ to their quotients ($C_{V/M}(\gamma_2(V/M))$ contains the image of
$C_V(\gamma_2(V))$), $e_V$ does not grow, and the left-hand side does not grow. The condition
depends on the target order $2^N$: in the search for candidates of order $\le 2^N$ it loses
nothing (with larger $N$ it is only weaker).

*For the parent itself* ($V = R$, $|R| = 2^{N-1}$) the inequality is sharp: $Q_{ab} = R_{ab}$
(since $n \in \gamma_2(Q)$), and (Г$_N$) requires, in particular, $\lambda_2(R_{ab}) \ge e_R + 1$:
for a parent of class 3 the abelianization must have two invariants larger than
$|\gamma_3(R)|$. On ancestors of order $2^{N-1-s}$ the margin is $2^s$ times larger.

Together with (Г) these are all the conditions checked by `prune.g` (`PR_Data`: fields `p3`,
`p4`, `p5` = (Г), `p5b` = (Г$_N$)). Cyclicity of $\gamma_3$ is part of (Г) (a cyclic
$K \ge \gamma_3$ is needed); class $\le 3$ is checked separately.

## Corollary: what remains at $2^{11}$

Substitute into (Г$_N$) the rough bounds $|K| \ge 2^{e_V}$ ($K \ge \gamma_3(V)$),
$|A \vee B \vee E_1| \ge 2^{e_V+1}$, $|A \vee V_{ab} \vee E_2| \ge 2^{d-2} \cdot 2^{2(e_V+1)}$
($V_{ab}$ has $d$ invariants, two of them replaced by $\ge e_V + 1$):

**Corollary 1.** If $Q$ is a candidate of order $2^N$, then every ancestor $V$ of it (and $R$) of
class 3 satisfies $d + 4e_V + 2 \le N$, where $2^{e_V} = |\gamma_3(V)|$. For $N = 11$: ancestors
of class 3 have $|\gamma_3(V)| = 2$ and $d \le 5$. In particular, for $d \ge 6$ all ancestors of
a candidate of order $2^{11}$, including $R$, have class $\le 2$, and for $d \le 5$ a parent of
class 3 has $|\gamma_3(R)| = 2$, that is, $|\gamma_3(Q)| = 4$. $\square$

Where (Г) does not work:

* **For $d = 2, 3$ condition (Г) is empty.** $\gamma_2(V) = \langle [x_i,x_j] \rangle \gamma_3(V)$,
  so with $K = \gamma_3(V)$ the rank of $\gamma_2(V)/K$ is at most $d(d-1)/2$; for $d = 2$ this is
  $1 = r_{\max}$, for $d = 3$ it is $3 = r_{\max}$. Only the cyclicity of $\gamma_3$ remains.
* **For $d \ge 4$ (Г) switches on late.** For (Г) to fail one needs
  $\mathrm{rk}\,\gamma_2(V) \ge r_{\max} + 1 = d + 1$, that is,
  $|V| \ge |V_{ab}|\,|\gamma_2(V)| \ge 2^{2d+1}$: for $d = 4$ this is order $\ge 2^9$, for $d = 5$
  $\ge 2^{11}$. For the $2^{11}$ search (vertices up to $2^{10}$) (Г) can cut only vertices of
  rank 4 of orders $2^9$ and $2^{10}$; on orders 32–512 it cut none.

What works is the budget (Г$_N$): on ancestors it cuts vertices with a large $\gamma_2$ or a
non-trivial $\gamma_3$ at large rank (Corollary 1), and on the parent itself it is sharp.

### One rank in pieces

From rank 4 on, one process is too long and too large, and `run_rank.sh` splits the walk into
pieces A/B/C/L (see its header). Every vertex has one place in the tree, so the pieces together
walk exactly the tree of one process. Three details matter for larger ranks:

* **Deferred steps** (`tree.g` `DEFERFILE`). `PqDescendants` builds the list of descendants of one
  step of a vertex whole, so a vertex of order 128 expanded by three steps at once can need many
  GB; in rank 4 stage A ran out of memory that way. Stage A therefore takes only the steps that
  stay below $2^{L_1}$ and writes every other (vertex, step) as a line
  `{"order":..,"code":..,"steps":[s]}`; stage B runs each such line as its own piece (`PD` at a
  time, GAP memory `GAPMEM_D`). A vertex read with `"steps"` is not counted again (the run that
  wrote it counted it).
* **Batches**: `STOP_AFTER_A=1`, `LIMIT=N` (at most N pieces per call, exit 3 while pieces remain),
  so that a long rank runs as a sequence of jobs over one `OUTDIR`; `TRACE` makes `tree.g` name
  each vertex before it is expanded.
* **Compact candidates** (`COMPACT=true`): a candidate line carries its presentation and the tests
  of `../pq1024/latb/export_pres.g`, ≈ 4 KB. With `COMPACT` `tree.g` writes only
  `{"order","id","rank","code","n"}` — the pc code of $Q$ (`CodePcGroup`) and the exponents of
  its involution $n$, ≈ 150 bytes — and `expand_compact.g` rebuilds the full line from it, the
  same bytes as without `COMPACT` (checked on the pieces of `../../boinc/README.md`); stage L
  does this before the lattice.
* **Shuffled stage C** (`CSEED=N`): the dump lists the children of one vertex together, so split
  in order a few pieces take most of the time (in rank 4 the heaviest piece of 40 vertices took
  40 times the median). `shuffle_split.py` orders the lines by `sha256(seed, line)` — the same on
  every machine — before the split and writes `Cin/split.json` (the seed and each piece's lines
  and hash). The stat counters are sums over vertices and every candidate belongs to the parent it
  was found on, so the result does not depend on how the vertices are grouped.

`run_r8.sh`: rank 8, whose first step from $2^8$ runs out of memory in `PqDescendants`, starts
instead from the groups of rank 8 of order 512 in SmallGroups (exactly the step-1 descendants of
$2^8$).

## Vertices without descendants

`PqDescendants` first builds $\mathrm{Aut}(V)$ and only then finds out that there are no
descendants or that all of them will be discarded. `steps.g` (`TS_Steps`) decides this in advance
from the $p$-cover of the vertex (one call of `PqPCover`, without $\mathrm{Aut}$) and leaves to
`tree.g` only the steps at which the descendants we need can occur.

**Notation.** $V$ is a vertex of $2$-class $c$, rank $d$, $|\gamma_3(V)| = 2^{e_V}$;
$V = F/K$, $F$ free of rank $d$; $V^* = F/[K,F]K^2$ (`PqPCover`); the multiplicator
$M = K/[K,F]K^2$ is the last $m$ elements of the pcgs of $V^*$; the nucleus $N = P_{c+1}(V^*) \le M$
(`PCentralSeries(V^*, 2)[c+1]`). Dimensions are over $\mathbb F_2$.

**Statement 4 (step-$s$ descendants).** Every immediate descendant $H$ of the vertex $V$ of step
$s$ is isomorphic to $V^*/U$, where $U \le M$, $|M : U| = 2^s$ and $NU = M$.

*Proof* — as Statement 1 in `../pq1024/README.md`, with $2^s$ instead of 2.
$P_{c+1}(H) \le \Phi(H)$, so $d(H) = d$, and preimages of the generators of $V$ give an
epimorphism $F \to H$ over $F \to V$ with kernel $L \le K$. $K/L = P_{c+1}(H)$ is central
($[P_{c+1}, H] \le P_{c+2}(H) = 1$) and elementary ($P_{c+1}^2 \le P_{c+2} = 1$), so
$[K,F]K^2 \le L$ and $H \cong V^*/U$, $U = L/[K,F]K^2$, $|M/U| = 2^s$. The terms of the lower
exponent-2 central series go to images: $P_{c+1}(V^*/U) = NU/U$; and $P_{c+1}(H) = M/U$ (this is
the layer of order $2^s$, $H/P_{c+1}(H) \cong V$). Hence $NU = M$. $\square$

Hence $s = \dim M/U = \dim N - \dim(N \cap U) \le \dim N$.

**Rule 0 (terminality).** If $N = 0$, there are no descendants of any step. *Proof:*
$NU = U \ne M$ for $s \ge 1$. $\square$

**Rule 1 (class $\le 3$).** A descendant of class $\le 3$ of step $s$ is possible only if
$s \le \nu_4 = \dim(N + G_4) - \dim G_4$, $G_4 = \gamma_4(V^*)$.
*Proof.* $\gamma_4(V) = 1$, hence $G_4 \le M$, and
$\gamma_4(V^*/U) = G_4U/U = 1 \iff G_4 \le U$. Then $N \cap U \supseteq N \cap G_4$ and
$s = \dim N - \dim(N \cap U) \le \dim N - \dim(N \cap G_4) = \nu_4$. $\square$

**Rule 2 (Corollary 1).** Suppose candidates of order $\le 2^{N}$ are sought (`LOGQ`). If
$d + 4(e_V + 1) + 2 > N$, then a descendant that the walk keeps lies over
$G = G_4 + (\gamma_3(V^*) \cap M)$, and the step is bounded by $\nu = \dim(N + G) - \dim G$.
*Proof.* Let $A_3 = \gamma_3(V^*) \cap M$, $H = V^*/U$. By the modular law
$\gamma_3(H) \cap (M/U) = (A_3 + U)/U$, and $\gamma_3(H)/(\gamma_3(H) \cap M/U) \cong \gamma_3(V)$;
hence $|\gamma_3(H)| = 2^{e_V}\,|(A_3+U)/U|$. If $A_3 \not\le U$, then $H$ has class 3 and
$e_H \ge e_V + 1$, that is, $d + 4e_H + 2 > N$. But the left-hand side is the rough lower bound
for the quantity `bud` of (Г$_N$) (section "Corollary": it is obtained from the invariants of $H$
themselves, without a candidate), so `PR_Data(H, N).p5b` is false, and the walk without the rules
would have discarded $H$ itself. So $A_3 \le U$ and $G_4 \le U$; the rest is as in Rule 1.
$\square$

**Rule 3 (the last level).** The walk does not expand descendants of order $2^{\mathrm{NMAX}}$;
they are needed only as parents, that is, with $2$-class $\ge 3$. A descendant has $2$-class
$c + 1$, so for $c = 1$ (elementary abelian $V$) the step $\mathrm{NMAX} - k$ is not needed.

All four rules discard only descendants that the walk without the rules would also have discarded
or not used. So the parents and the candidates are the same; only the counter `vert` on the last
level changes (descendants of $p$-class 2, which did not lead anywhere before either, are not
generated).

## The p-cover without pq

`PqPCover` (ANUPQ) costs ≈ 22–28 ms per group of order 512, and it is called once per vertex by
`steps.g` and once per parent by the cover search (`../pq1024/cover.g`, `MC_Data`). A profile of
rank-6 vertices of order 512 (`prof_vertex.g`): 15 ms of it is the GAP side of ANUPQ (writing the
presentation, reading the result file back and rebuilding the group), 7 ms the `pq` process. So
one `pq` session per piece instead of a process per call would save at most ≈ 7 ms; replacing
`PqPCover` altogether saves all of it. `pcover.g` does that with GAP's two-cohomology.

**Statement 5.** Let $G$ be a finite 2-group, $r = \dim H^2(G, \mathbb F_2)$, $c_1, \dots, c_r$
2-cocycles $G \times G \to \mathbb F_2$ whose classes form a basis of $H^2(G, \mathbb F_2)$, and
$E$ the extension of $G$ by the trivial module $V = \mathbb F_2^r$ with cocycle $(c_1, \dots, c_r)$.
Then $E \cong G^* = F/[K,F]K^2$ by an isomorphism over $G$ (so that $V$ goes to the multiplicator
$M = K/[K,F]K^2$).

*Proof.* Write $G = F/K$ with $F$ free of rank $d = d(G)$, so $K \le \Phi(F)$. In the five-term
sequence for $1 \to K \to F \to G \to 1$ with coefficients $\mathbb F_2$, the restriction
$H^1(F) \to H^1(K)^F$ is zero (every homomorphism $F \to \mathbb F_2$ kills $K \le \Phi(F)$), and
$H^2(F) = 0$; hence the transgression is an isomorphism
$\mathrm{Hom}_F(K, \mathbb F_2) = \mathrm{Hom}(M, \mathbb F_2) \cong H^2(G, \mathbb F_2)$, the class of
the extension $1 \to M/U \to F/U \to G \to 1$ corresponding to the functional with kernel $U$.
So the extension $G^*$ of $G$ by $M$ has a class in $H^2(G, M)$ whose image under each functional
$\lambda \in \mathrm{Hom}(M, \mathbb F_2)$ is the transgression of $\lambda$; with the basis of
$M$ dual to $c_1, \dots, c_r$ that class is $(c_1, \dots, c_r)$ up to coboundaries. Extensions with
cohomologous cocycles are equivalent, so $E \cong G^*$ over $G$. $\square$

GAP's `Extension(G, V, c)` returns $E$ as a pc group whose pcgs is that of $G$ followed by the $r$
module generators; so, as with `PqPCover`, the multiplicator is spanned by the last $r$ pcgs
elements, and `steps.g` and `cover.g` read it the same way. `PC_Cover` chooses: `PqPCover` by
default, `PC_PCover` when `OWNCOVER` is true (environment `PQ2048_OWNCOVER=1`, so that
`run_rank.sh` and its pieces need no new argument).

## Step-1 children without pq

`children.g` (`CH_Step1`, flag `OWNDESC`, environment `PQ2048_OWNDESC=1`) builds the step-1
children of a vertex $V$ without `PqDescendants`: from the $p$-cover $V^*$ (`PC_Cover`), the
multiplicator $M$, the nucleus $N$ and $G_4 = \gamma_4(V^*)$,

* the functionals $f$ on $M$ that vanish on $G_4$ and not on $N$ — exactly the allowable
  hyperplanes $U = \ker f$ whose quotient $V^*/U$ has class $\le 3$ (Statement 4 and rule 1 of
  "Vertices without descendants"; the walk discards every child of class $\ge 4$ uncounted, so
  nothing it counts is lost);
* their orbits under the action of $\mathrm{Aut}(V)$ lifted to $V^*$ (`MC_AutMats` of
  `../pq1024/cover.g`, the same as for the candidates); by O'Brien's theorem on the standard
  algorithm two allowable hyperplanes give isomorphic descendants iff they lie in one orbit;
* one child $V^*/U$ per orbit, as a pc quotient of $V^*$ (`MC_Quotient`) — no fp presentation and
  no `pq` process.

The functionals are enumerated as vectors of $\mathbb F_2^k$, $k = \dim M - \dim G_4$, with a bit
list of the ones already met, stopping when the orbits found cover all $2^k - 2^{k - \mathrm{rk}}$
allowable ones. For $k > 22$ (`CH_KMAX`) `CH_Step1` returns `fail` and `CH_Desc` calls
`PqDescendants`; steps $\ge 2$ always go to `PqDescendants`. `tree.g` calls `CH_Desc` instead of
`MS_Desc` (one word changed; without the flag it is `MS_Desc`).

## Not proved

Conditions that look true but are **not proved** and are not used for pruning:

1. **Step-1 descendants of vertices of $p$-class 2, rank 5, order 512 do not pass (Г$_{11}$).**
   In the sample: 0 of 3028 (40 vertices). *In doubt after the sample with the step rules*
   (section "Vertices without descendants": 254 expanded vertices of rank 5 gave 2008 such
   descendants; the $p$-class of these vertices is not recorded there). If this is a theorem (for
   example, via Corollary 1: a parent of class 3 with $d = 5$ must have
   $R_{ab} \cong \mathbb Z/4^2 \oplus \mathbb Z/2^3$, $|X| = 4$,
   $\gamma_2 \cap Z = \gamma_3 \cong \mathbb Z/4$ — all rough bounds become equalities; a parent of
   class 2 needs a separate argument), then rank 5 needs to be expanded only from vertices of
   $p$-class $\ge 3$ (≈ 48 thousand instead of 5.9 million), and rank 5 costs hours, not months.
   This is the main candidate for the next proof.
2. **Prediction from the cover of a vertex.** Condition (Г$_{11}$) on a child $R = V^*/U$ is
   checked only after `PqDescendants(V)`. A two-level linear test on the $p$-cover of $V$ (as in
   the $p$-cover search for $Q = R^*/U$) could discard $V$ without $\mathrm{Aut}(V)$; not done.
3. **A reduction for central products.** Large rank with cyclic centre comes from central products
   with extraspecial groups ($H \circ E$). If $D_5(H \circ E) = 1$ followed from $D_5(H) = 1$,
   ranks $\ge 5$ would drop out entirely. Functoriality gives only the inclusion of the image,
   $D_5(G) \twoheadrightarrow$ does not follow; the statement is not proved and was not checked.
4. **$\mathrm{rk}\,\gamma_2(Q) \le d$** is false: on order 512
   $\mathrm{rk}\,\gamma_2(Q) = d + 1$ occurs (`results/small/cands_512.json`, `r2_minus_d` = 1), so
   the "$+1$" in (Г3) is needed.
5. **Known results on $D_4$ for small class.** A counterexample must have $D_4(Q) \ne 1$. If it
   were proved that $D_4 = \gamma_4$ for 2-generated groups or under some condition on $Q_{ab}$
   (the Rips / Tahara / Losey / Hartley direction, survey by Mikhailov–Passi, LNM 1952), this would
   give pruning by rank or by the type of $Q_{ab}$ ($Q_{ab} = R_{ab}$ is visible on the parent).
   We have **not checked** such statements in the sources †; the paper in preparation uses from
   this area only $D_4^2 \subseteq \gamma_4$ (Passi), Tahara's bound and Gupta–Tahara for odd $p$.

† — not checked against the source.
